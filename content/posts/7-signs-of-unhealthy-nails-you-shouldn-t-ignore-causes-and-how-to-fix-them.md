## 7 Signs Of Unhealthy Nails You Shouldn't Ignore: Causes & How To Fix Them

Your nails are not just for beauty. They are like a window to your health. Healthy nails are smooth, pink, strong, and shiny. But when your nails change color, texture, or shape, it can be your body's way of telling you that something is wrong.

Many people ignore nail changes, thinking they are just cosmetic. They cover yellow nails with polish, hide peeling nails with acrylics, and ignore ridges and white spots. But ignoring these signs can make the problem worse.

Some nail problems are simple and easy to fix at home, like dryness or staining from polish. Others can be a sign of nutrient deficiency, infection, or health issues that need proper attention.

In this complete guide, you will learn the 7 signs of unhealthy nails you should never ignore, what each sign means, what causes it, how to fix it at home, and when you should see a doctor. By the end, you will know how to read your nails and keep them healthy.

## What Do Healthy Nails Look Like?

Before we learn about unhealthy signs, let us understand what healthy nails look like.

Healthy nails are smooth, without ridges, grooves, or pits. They are pinkish in color, with a white tip. They are strong, flexible, and do not break easily. Cuticles are soft, smooth, and not dry or cracked. Nails grow steadily, about 3mm per month.

If your nails do not look like this, do not panic. Most nail problems are common and fixable. The key is to notice them early and take care of them.

## Sign 1: Ridged Nails - May Signal Nutrient Deficiency

Ridged nails have vertical or horizontal lines running across the nail. Vertical ridges run from cuticle to tip and are quite common, especially as we age. Horizontal ridges, also called Beau's lines, run across the nail and are more concerning.

### What Causes Ridged Nails?

Vertical ridges are often caused by aging, dryness, and lack of moisture. As we age, our nails produce less oil and become drier, which causes ridges. They can also be caused by nutrient deficiency, especially lack of iron, zinc, and protein.

Horizontal ridges can be caused by trauma to the nail, illness, high fever, stress, or nutrient deficiency. They appear when nail growth is temporarily interrupted.

### How To Fix Ridged Nails:

Keep nails moisturized with cuticle oil daily. Do not buff ridges aggressively, as this thins nails. Eat foods rich in iron, zinc, protein, and biotin, like eggs, spinach, nuts, and lentils. Drink enough water. Be gentle with nails and avoid harsh chemicals.

If horizontal ridges appear suddenly on all nails and do not go away, see a doctor, as they can be a sign of a health issue.

## Sign 2: White Spots - Could Be A Sign Of Past Injury Or Mineral Deficiency

White spots on nails are very common. They are small white dots or lines on the nail plate. Most people get them at some point.

### What Causes White Spots?

The most common cause is minor injury to the nail matrix, like bumping your nail, closing it in a door, or aggressive manicuring. The injury damages the nail cells, and a white spot appears as the nail grows out.

White spots can also be caused by zinc deficiency, calcium deficiency, or allergic reaction to nail polish or nail products. In rare cases, they can be a sign of fungal infection.

### How To Fix White Spots:

White spots from injury usually go away on their own as the nail grows out. It takes 2 to 3 months. Do not pick or try to remove them. Eat zinc-rich foods like pumpkin seeds, nuts, lentils, and chickpeas. Eat calcium-rich foods like yogurt, milk, and almonds. Be gentle with nails and avoid hitting them. Use a gentle, hypoallergenic nail polish if you are allergic to polish.

If white spots cover the whole nail, are spreading, or come with other symptoms, see a doctor.

## Sign 3: Peeling Nails - Often Caused By Dryness Or Harsh Products

Peeling nails are nails that split into layers at the tip. The top layer peels off like paper, making nails thin, weak, and rough.

### What Causes Peeling Nails?

Peeling is most commonly caused by dryness. When nails lose moisture, the layers separate. Frequent hand washing, hot water, harsh soaps, cleaning chemicals, and acetone remover all dry out nails.

Peeling can also be caused by over-filing, using nails as tools, peeling off gel polish, and lack of biotin and iron in diet.

### How To Fix Peeling Nails:

Keep nails moisturized with cuticle oil 2 to 3 times a day. Wear gloves while cleaning and washing dishes. Use acetone-free remover. File nails gently in one direction with a fine file. Do not peel off gel polish, always remove properly. Eat biotin-rich foods like eggs, nuts, and sweet potatoes. Keep nails short until peeling stops.

Peeling usually improves in 2 to 4 weeks with consistent care.

## Sign 4: Yellow Nails - May Indicate Fungal Infection Or Other Issues

Yellow nails are nails that look yellow, dull, or stained. They are one of the most common nail problems.

### What Causes Yellow Nails?

The most common cause is frequent nail polish use, especially dark colors, without base coat. The pigments stain the nail plate yellow.

Yellow nails can also be caused by smoking, harsh chemicals, poor diet, and lack of nail care. In some cases, they can be a sign of fungal infection, especially if nails are also thick, crumbly, and have a bad smell.

### How To Fix Yellow Nails:

Take a break from polish for 1 to 2 weeks. Always use base coat before applying color. Use lemon and olive oil soak once a week to brighten nails. Mix 1 tablespoon lemon juice with 2 tablespoons olive oil, soak for 10 minutes, rinse, and moisturize. Apply cuticle oil daily. Wear gloves while cleaning. Eat foods rich in vitamin E and vitamin C.

If yellowing comes with thickening, crumbling, or bad smell, and does not improve in 2 months, see a doctor for fungal treatment.

## Sign 5: Brittle Nails - Can Be A Sign Of Vitamin Or Moisture Lack

Brittle nails break, crack, and split very easily. They feel dry, thin, and weak. You cannot grow them long because they break before they grow.

### What Causes Brittle Nails?

Brittle nails are most commonly caused by lack of moisture. Dry air, winter, hot water, harsh soaps, and hand sanitizer dry out nails and make them brittle.

They can also be caused by lack of biotin, iron, protein, and vitamins in diet, overuse of gel and acrylics, aging, and thyroid issues.

### How To Fix Brittle Nails:

Apply cuticle oil and hand cream daily, especially at night. Use coconut oil or olive oil soaks 3 times a week. Eat biotin and protein-rich foods like eggs, salmon, nuts, and lentils. Drink 7 to 8 glasses of water daily. Avoid harsh chemicals and wear gloves. Take a break from gel and acrylics for 2 to 4 weeks.

Brittle nails usually improve in 4 to 6 weeks with consistent care.

## Sign 6: Discolored Nails - May Point To Fungal Infection Or Other Health Issues

Discolored nails can be yellow, brown, green, blue, black, or white. Any sudden change in nail color that is not from polish should not be ignored.

### What Causes Discolored Nails?

Discolored nails can be caused by fungal infection, bacterial infection, injury, staining from polish or chemicals, smoking, and certain medications. Dark lines or spots under nails can be more serious and should be checked by a doctor immediately.

Green nails can be caused by bacterial infection, especially if nails are exposed to moisture for long periods. Black nails can be caused by trauma, like hitting your nail hard.

### How To Fix Discolored Nails:

First, identify the cause. If it is staining from polish, take a break and use whitening remedies like lemon soak. If it is from injury, it will grow out in a few months. Keep nails clean and dry to prevent infection. Do not cover discolored nails with polish to hide them, as this can make infection worse.

If discoloration is spreading, painful, or comes with swelling, pus, or dark lines, see a doctor immediately. Do not try to treat serious discoloration at home.

## Sign 7: Thin Nails - Could Be Due To Poor Diet Or Excessive Use Of Chemicals

Thin nails bend easily, feel papery, and break with the slightest pressure. They are often a result of damage.

### What Causes Thin Nails?

Thin nails are most commonly caused by over-filing, aggressive manicures, and peeling off gel or acrylics. Peeling removes layers of the natural nail and makes it thin.

They can also be caused by poor diet, lack of protein and biotin, excessive use of chemicals, and aging.

### How To Fix Thin Nails:

Give nails a break from gel and acrylics for 2 to 4 weeks. Apply cuticle oil 3 to 4 times a day. Use a gentle nail strengthener with keratin. Keep nails short and rounded. Eat protein-rich foods like eggs, chicken, lentils, and yogurt. Avoid filing aggressively and use a fine glass file.

Thin nails take time to recover because new healthy nail needs to grow out. Full recovery can take 3 to 6 months, but you will see improvement in 3 to 4 weeks.

## Healthy Nails Tips - How To Keep Your Nails Healthy

Keep Your Nails Moisturized: Apply cuticle oil daily and hand cream after every hand wash. Moisturized nails are strong, flexible, and healthy.

**Eat A Balanced Diet:** Eat foods rich in biotin, protein, iron, zinc, and vitamins. Eggs, spinach, salmon, nuts, and citrus fruits are excellent for nails.

**Avoid Harsh Chemicals:** Wear gloves while cleaning, use acetone-free remover, and avoid harsh soaps. Harsh chemicals dry out and damage nails.

**Give Your Nails A Little Break:** Take a break from polish, gel, and acrylics every few weeks. Let your nails breathe, rehydrate, and recover.

**Be Gentle:** Do not use nails as tools, do not bite or pick, do not over-file, and do not peel polish off. Gentle care prevents most nail problems.

**Keep Nails Clean And Dry:** Clean under nails gently, keep them dry, and avoid long soaking in water. Clean, dry nails are less likely to get infected.

**Drink Enough Water:** Hydrated nails are healthy nails. Drink 7 to 8 glasses of water daily.

## When Should You See A Doctor?

Most nail problems can be fixed at home with care and patience. But see a doctor if you notice:

Nails are very thick, crumbly, or separating from the nail bed. Pain, swelling, pus, or bad smell around nails. Dark lines or spots under nails. Sudden change in nail color or shape. Nails do not improve after 2 months of care. You have diabetes or circulation problems and notice nail changes.

Do not ignore these signs. Early treatment can prevent bigger problems.

## Frequently Asked Questions - FAQs

### 1. What are the signs of unhealthy nails?

The main signs of unhealthy nails are ridged nails, white spots, peeling nails, yellow nails, brittle nails, discolored nails, and thin nails. Other signs include dry cuticles, hangnails, slow growth, and nails that break easily. If you notice any of these signs, start gentle nail care and improve your diet.

### 2. Why are my nails ridged and brittle?

Ridged and brittle nails are usually caused by dryness, aging, lack of moisture, and nutrient deficiency, especially lack of iron, zinc, biotin, and protein. Harsh chemicals, over-filing, and frequent gel or acrylic use can also cause ridges and brittleness. Regular cuticle oil, a healthy diet, and gentle care can fix most ridged and brittle nails.

### 3. What causes white spots on nails?

White spots on nails are most commonly caused by minor injury to the nail, like bumping or hitting your nail. They can also be caused by zinc deficiency or allergic reaction to nail products. White spots from injury usually grow out in 2 to 3 months. Eat zinc-rich foods and be gentle with your nails.

### 4. How can I fix peeling and yellow nails?

To fix peeling and yellow nails, take a break from polish, apply cuticle oil daily, use acetone-free remover, wear gloves while cleaning, and use lemon and olive oil soak once a week for yellowing. Eat biotin-rich foods and keep nails short until peeling stops. Most peeling and yellowing improves in 2 to 4 weeks.

### 5. Are thin nails after acrylics normal?

Yes, thin nails after acrylics are very common. Filing and peeling off acrylics removes layers of the natural nail, making it thin and weak. Give nails a 2 to 4 week break, apply cuticle oil 3 to 4 times a day, use a nail strengthener, and keep nails short. Full recovery takes 3 to 6 months.

### 6. When should I see a doctor for unhealthy nails?

You should see a doctor if your nails are very thick, crumbly, or separating, if there is pain, swelling, pus, or bad smell, if you see dark lines under nails, if discoloration spreads quickly, or if nails do not improve after 2 months of care. If you have diabetes or circulation problems, see a doctor sooner.

## Final Thoughts

Your nails tell a story about your health. Ridged, peeling, yellow, brittle, discolored, thin nails, and white spots are not just cosmetic problems. They are signs that your nails need attention, care, and nourishment.

The good news is, most unhealthy nails can be fixed with simple habits, keep them moisturized, eat a balanced diet, avoid harsh chemicals, be gentle, and give them breaks from polish and acrylics.

Start paying attention to your nails today. Notice the signs early, take care of them consistently, and do not ignore changes that do not go away.
