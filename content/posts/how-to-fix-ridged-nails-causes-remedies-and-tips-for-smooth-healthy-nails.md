## How To Fix Ridged Nails: Causes, Remedies & Tips For Smooth, Healthy & Beautiful Nails

Do your nails look rough, uneven, and bumpy instead of smooth and shiny? Do you see fine lines running up and down your nails? If yes, you have ridged nails.

Ridged nails are one of the most common nail problems. They make polish look uneven, make nails look dull and aged, and can be a sign that your nails need more care and nutrients.

The good news is, most ridged nails can be fixed at home with simple care, good nutrition, and gentle habits. You do not need expensive treatments. You just need to understand why ridges happen and how to fix them correctly.

In this complete guide, you will learn what ridged nails are, the difference between vertical and horizontal ridges, 5 common causes, 6 simple remedies to fix them, what not to do, and when to see a doctor. By the end, you will know how to get smooth, healthy, and beautiful nails again.

## What Are Ridged Nails?

Ridged nails are nails that have lines, grooves, or ridges running across the nail surface, instead of being smooth.

Healthy nails are smooth, pink, and shiny. Ridged nails feel rough when you run your finger over them. They look dull, uneven, and sometimes brittle. Polish does not go on smoothly on ridged nails and chips faster.

## There are two types of ridges:

**Vertical Ridges**: These are lines that run from the cuticle to the tip, up and down the nail. They are the most common type. They are usually harmless and caused by aging, dryness, and nutrient deficiency. Almost everyone gets some vertical ridges as they get older.

**Horizontal Ridges**: These are lines that run across the nail, from side to side. They are also called Beau's lines. They are less common and more concerning. They appear when nail growth is temporarily interrupted by illness, high fever, stress, injury, or nutrient deficiency.

It is important to know which type you have. Vertical ridges can usually be fixed at home. Horizontal ridges that appear suddenly on all nails should be checked by a doctor.

## Why It Happens? 5 Common Causes Of Ridged Nails

## Cause 1: Aging - The Most Common Cause

Aging is the number one cause of vertical ridged nails. As we age, our body produces less natural oil and moisture. Nails become drier, thinner, and less smooth. The nail matrix, where new nail cells are made, also slows down with age, which causes ridges.

This is completely normal. Just like wrinkles on skin, ridges on nails are a natural part of aging. But with good care, you can minimize them and keep nails smooth for longer.

**How To Fix**: Keep nails moisturized daily with cuticle oil and hand cream. Eat a healthy diet. Be gentle with nails. Aging ridges cannot be completely removed, but they can be greatly improved.

## Cause 2: Nutrient Deficiency

Nutrient deficiency is a major cause of ridged nails, especially lack of biotin, iron, zinc, protein, and vitamins.

Biotin helps produce keratin, the protein that makes up nails. Without enough biotin, nails become ridged, weak, and brittle.

Iron deficiency can cause ridges, pale nails, and spoon-shaped nails. Zinc deficiency can cause ridges, white spots, and slow growth. Protein deficiency makes nails thin and weak.

If you have a poor diet, crash diet, or do not eat enough protein and vegetables, your nails will show it first.

**How To Fix**: Eat foods rich in biotin, iron, zinc, and protein. Eggs, spinach, salmon, almonds, lentils, chickpeas, and sweet potatoes are excellent. Drink enough water. Consider supplements only after consulting your doctor.

## Cause 3: Frequent Use Of Polish

Frequent use of nail polish, gel, and acrylics can cause ridged nails. Polish, especially dark colors, dries out nails. Gel and acrylics require filing and buffing, which thins nails and creates ridges. Acetone remover also dries out nails badly.

If you always wear polish and never give nails a break, your nails never get a chance to rehydrate and recover. They become dry, ridged, and weak.

**How To Fix**: Take 2 to 3 days break from polish every week. Use base coat always. Use acetone-free remover. Remove gel properly, never peel it off. Give nails 2 to 4 weeks break after gel or acrylics.

## Cause 4: Harsh Chemicals

Harsh chemicals in cleaning products, detergents, dishwashing soaps, bleach, and hand sanitizers dry out nails and cause ridges. They strip natural oils and damage the nail plate.

Even frequent hand washing with harsh soap can cause ridges over time.

**How To Fix**: Wear rubber gloves while cleaning and washing dishes. Use mild, moisturizing soap. Use alcohol-free hand sanitizer. Apply hand cream after every hand wash.

## Cause 5: Underlying Health Issues

Sometimes ridged nails can be a sign of underlying health issues like thyroid problems, anemia, diabetes, eczema, psoriasis, or severe stress. Horizontal ridges can appear after high fever, illness, or major stress.

If ridges appear suddenly, are on all nails, are deep and painful, or come with other symptoms like fatigue, hair fall, pale skin, or weight changes, you should see a doctor.

**How To Fix**: Do not ignore sudden, severe ridges. See a doctor for proper diagnosis. Treat the underlying issue. In the meantime, take gentle care of your nails.

## 6 Simple Remedies To Fix Ridged Nails

## Remedy 1: Moisturize Regularly - Use Cuticle Oil Or Hand Cream

Moisturizing is the most important remedy for ridged nails. Dry nails are ridged nails. Moisturized nails are smooth nails.

Apply cuticle oil 2 to 3 times a day. Use jojoba oil, coconut oil, almond oil, or vitamin E oil. Massage into nails and cuticles for 1 minute per hand. Massage improves blood circulation to the nail matrix, which helps new, smooth nail grow.

Apply hand cream after every hand wash. Choose a thick cream with shea butter, glycerin, or ceramides.

For extra dry, ridged nails, try the overnight trick. Apply cuticle oil and hand cream before bed, wear cotton gloves, and sleep. You will wake up with softer, smoother nails.

Do this daily for at least 4 to 6 weeks. Consistency is key.

## Remedy 2: Get Essential Nutrients - Biotin, Zinc & Protein-Rich Foods

Nourish your nails from inside. Eat a balanced diet rich in biotin, zinc, iron, and protein.

Eat 1 to 2 eggs daily for biotin and protein. Eat salmon 2 times a week for omega-3 and protein. Eat spinach 3 to 4 times a week for iron. Eat a handful of almonds and pumpkin seeds daily for zinc and vitamin E. Eat lentils, chickpeas, and beans for protein and iron. Eat sweet potatoes for vitamin A. Eat oranges for vitamin C, which helps absorb iron.

Drink 7 to 8 glasses of water daily. Hydrated nails are smooth nails.

If you are considering biotin supplements, please consult your doctor first. Do not take high doses without professional advice. Food is always the best source.

## Remedy 3: Keep Your Body Hydrated - Drink Plenty Of Water

Dehydration makes nails dry, brittle, and ridged. When your body is dehydrated, your nails get moisture last, after vital organs. That is why dry, ridged nails are often a sign of dehydration.

Drink at least 7 to 8 glasses of water daily. Eat water-rich foods like cucumber, watermelon, oranges, and yogurt. Avoid excessive caffeine and alcohol, as they dehydrate the body.

Hydration from inside + moisturizing from outside = smooth nails.

## Remedy 4: File Gently - Use A Fine Nail File Instead Of Buffing

Many people try to fix ridges by buffing them away aggressively. This is a big mistake. Buffing thins nails, makes them weaker, and causes more ridges and breakage.

Instead, file gently with a fine-grit glass file. File in one direction only, from side to center. Do not saw back and forth. Do not file wet nails.

If you want to smooth ridges for polish, use a very fine buffer very lightly, only once a month, and only if nails are strong. If nails are weak, do not buff at all. Just use a ridge-filling base coat before polish.

Ridge-filling base coat is a great trick. It fills in ridges, makes nails look smooth, and helps polish go on evenly. Always use it if you have ridged nails.

## Remedy 5: Avoid Harsh Products - Choose Acetone-Free Remover

Harsh products make ridges worse. Switch to gentle products.

Use acetone-free nail polish remover. Acetone dries out nails badly. Use 3-free or 5-free nail polish. Use mild, moisturizing soap. Use alcohol-free hand sanitizer.

Always use base coat before color. Base coat protects nails from staining and damage.

Wear gloves while cleaning. Do not expose nails to harsh chemicals.

Gentle products = healthy, smooth nails.

## Remedy 6: Try A Nail Mask - With Natural Oils Or Petroleum Jelly

Nail masks give deep nourishment to ridged nails. Try once a week.

**Coconut Oil Mask**: Warm 2 tablespoons coconut oil. Soak nails for 10 to 15 minutes. Massage remaining oil into nails. Leave overnight. Do 2 times a week.

**Olive Oil And Lemon Mask**: Mix 2 tablespoons olive oil with 1 tablespoon lemon juice. Soak nails for 10 minutes. Rinse and moisturize. Lemon brightens nails, olive oil nourishes.

**Petroleum Jelly Mask**: Apply a thick layer of petroleum jelly to nails and cuticles before bed. Wear cotton gloves. Leave overnight. Petroleum jelly seals in moisture and makes nails smooth. Do 2 to 3 times a week.

**Vitamin E Mask**: Open 1 vitamin E capsule, apply oil to nails, massage for 2 minutes, leave overnight. Vitamin E repairs damaged nails.

**Choose one mask and do it consistently for 4 weeks.**

## Extra Tips For Ridged Nails

**Take Biotin Supplements If Needed**: If your doctor recommends, take biotin supplements. Biotin can improve nail thickness and reduce ridges. Do not self-medicate. Consult your doctor first.

**Avoid Excessive Use Of Nail Polish**: Give nails breaks. Do not leave polish on for more than 7 days. Remove gently.

**Keep Nails Short If They Are Weak**: Short nails break less and are easier to care for. Keep them short and rounded until ridges improve. Then grow them longer.

**Do Not Use Nails As Tools**: Using nails to open cans, scratch, or peel damages them and causes ridges. Use proper tools.

**Protect Nails In Winter**: Cold, dry air makes ridges worse. Wear gloves, use thicker cream, and apply cuticle oil more often in winter.

**Be Patient**: Nails grow slowly. It takes 3 to 6 months for fully new, smooth nail to grow out. You will see improvement in 3 to 4 weeks, but full results take time. Take monthly photos to track progress.

## Common Mistakes To Avoid With Ridged Nails

**Mistake 1:** Buffing ridges aggressively. This thins nails and makes them worse.

**Mistake 2:** Skipping moisturizer. Dry nails always stay ridged.

**Mistake 3:** Peeling off gel polish. This removes layers of nail.

**Mistake 4:** Using acetone remover daily. This dries out nails.

**Mistake 5:** Ignoring horizontal ridges. Horizontal ridges can be a sign of health issues. See a doctor.

**Mistake 6:** Expecting overnight results. Healthy nails take time.

Avoid these mistakes and your nails will become smoother.

## Frequently Asked Questions - FAQs

### 1. What causes ridged nails?

Ridged nails are caused by aging, nutrient deficiency, frequent use of polish, harsh chemicals, and underlying health issues. Vertical ridges are usually caused by aging and dryness. Horizontal ridges can be caused by illness, high fever, stress, or nutrient deficiency. Most ridges are harmless and can be fixed with moisturizing and good nutrition.

### 2. How do I get rid of ridges on my nails?

To get rid of ridges on your nails, apply cuticle oil 2 to 3 times a day, eat biotin, zinc, and protein-rich foods, drink enough water, file gently with a fine file, use acetone-free remover, and try a weekly nail mask with coconut oil or petroleum jelly. Use a ridge-filling base coat before polish for instant smoothness. Be consistent for 4 to 6 weeks.

### 3. Are ridged nails a sign of vitamin deficiency?

Ridged nails can be a sign of vitamin and mineral deficiency, especially lack of biotin, iron, zinc, and protein. If you have many ridges along with brittle nails, hair fall, and fatigue, you might be low in these nutrients. Eat a balanced diet with eggs, spinach, salmon, nuts, and lentils. Consult your doctor if ridges are severe or sudden.

### 4. Should I buff my ridged nails?

No, you should not buff ridged nails aggressively. Buffing thins nails, makes them weaker, and causes more breakage. If you want to smooth ridges, use a very fine buffer very lightly, only once a month, and only if nails are strong. Better to use a ridge-filling base coat before polish, which fills ridges without damaging nails.

### 5. Can I wear nail polish if I have ridged nails?

Yes, you can wear nail polish if you have ridged nails, but always use a ridge-filling base coat first. Base coat fills ridges and makes polish go on smoothly. Take 2 to 3 days break from polish every week, use acetone-free remover, and moisturize daily. Give nails breaks to rehydrate and recover.

### 6. When should I see a doctor for ridged nails?

You should see a doctor if ridges are horizontal and appear suddenly on all nails, if ridges are deep and painful, if nails are also discolored, thick, or separating, or if you have other symptoms like fatigue, hair fall, or weight changes. Horizontal ridges can be a sign of health issues that need proper diagnosis.
