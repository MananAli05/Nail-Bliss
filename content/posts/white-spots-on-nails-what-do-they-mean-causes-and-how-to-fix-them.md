## White Spots On Nails: What Do They Mean? Causes & How To Fix Them

Have you ever looked at your nails and noticed small white spots or dots? You are not alone. White spots on nails are one of the most common nail problems, and almost everyone gets them at some point.

Most people panic when they see white spots. They think it is a calcium deficiency, a serious disease, or something dangerous. But in most cases, white spots are harmless and go away on their own as the nail grows out.

However, sometimes white spots can be a sign of nutrient deficiency, injury, infection, or allergy. That is why it is important to understand what white spots mean, what causes them, and how to fix them.

In this complete guide, you will learn what white spots on nails are, 5 common causes, what they mean, how to treat them at home, how to prevent them, and when you should see a doctor. By the end, you will know exactly what to do next time you see a white spot.

## What Are White Spots On Nails?

White spots on nails are called leukonychia in medical terms. Leukonychia means white nail. It appears as small white dots, lines, or patches on the nail plate.

## There are three types of white spots:

**True Leukonychia**: White spots are on the nail plate itself. They move with the nail as it grows. They do not disappear when you press on the nail. This is the most common type and is usually caused by injury.

**Apparent Leukonychia**: White spots are on the nail bed underneath, not on the nail plate. They fade when you press on the nail. This type can be a sign of health issues.

**Pseudoleukonychia**: White spots are on the surface of the nail, caused by nail polish, chemicals, or fungal infection. They can be wiped or filed away.

Most white spots you see are true leukonychia, caused by minor injury. They are harmless and grow out in 2 to 3 months.

## Are White Spots On Nails Dangerous?

In most cases, no. White spots on nails are usually harmless. They are not painful, they do not spread, and they go away on their own.

But you should pay attention if white spots are covering the whole nail, appearing on all nails at once, spreading or getting bigger, accompanied by pain, swelling, or thickening, or not going away after 3 months.

In these cases, white spots can be a sign of a more serious issue and you should see a doctor.

For most people, a few small white dots on one or two nails are completely normal and nothing to worry about.

## Cause 1: Minor Injury Or Trauma - The Most Common Cause

The number one cause of white spots on nails is minor injury or trauma to the nail matrix.

The nail matrix is the area at the base of your nail, under the cuticle, where new nail cells are made. If you bump your nail, close it in a door, bite your nails, pick at cuticles, or have an aggressive manicure, you can injure the matrix.

The injury damages the nail cells, and as the nail grows out, a white spot appears. You might not even remember the injury because the spot appears weeks later when the nail has grown out.

**Examples of minor injuries**: Hitting your nail on a table, closing finger in a drawer, aggressive pushing back of cuticles, biting nails, using nails as tools, picking off gel polish.

**How To Fix**: White spots from injury do not need treatment. They will grow out on their own in 2 to 3 months. Just be gentle with your nails, do not pick or bite, and apply cuticle oil daily to keep nails healthy.

**Prevention**: Be gentle with nails, do not use nails as tools, avoid aggressive manicures, wear gloves while doing heavy work.

## Cause 2: Nutrient Deficiency - Zinc, Calcium, Biotin

Many people believe white spots are always caused by calcium deficiency. This is a myth. White spots are rarely caused by calcium deficiency alone. They are more commonly linked to zinc deficiency.

Zinc is essential for nail growth and repair. Zinc deficiency can cause white spots, slow nail growth, and brittle nails. Other nutrients that affect nails include biotin, iron, and protein.

Signs of zinc deficiency include white spots on nails, slow wound healing, hair fall, and weak immunity. If you have many white spots on multiple nails along with these symptoms, you might be low in zinc.

**How To Fix**: Eat zinc-rich foods like pumpkin seeds, chickpeas, lentils, nuts, cashews, yogurt, eggs, and chicken. Eat biotin-rich foods like eggs, almonds, sweet potatoes, and oats. Eat calcium-rich foods like milk, yogurt, cheese, and almonds. Eat a balanced diet with plenty of protein, fruits, and vegetables.

Do not take high-dose zinc or calcium supplements without consulting your doctor. Too much zinc can also cause problems. Food is always the best source.

## Cause 3: Fungal Infection

White spots can sometimes be caused by a fungal infection, called superficial white onychomycosis. This is a type of nail fungus that causes white, chalky, powdery patches on the nail surface.

Unlike injury spots, fungal white spots are on the surface, can be scraped off, and tend to spread. Nails may also become thick, crumbly, yellow, and have a bad smell.

Fungal infections are more common on toenails than fingernails, because feet are warm and moist. But they can happen on fingernails too, especially if nails are exposed to water for long periods.

**How To Fix**: Keep nails clean and dry. Do not share nail tools. Avoid wearing wet gloves or socks for long. Use antifungal treatment as prescribed by a doctor. Do not cover infected nails with polish, as it can make infection worse.

If you think you have a fungal infection, see a dermatologist. Fungal infections need proper treatment and do not go away with home remedies alone.

## Cause 4: Allergies Or Skin Conditions

White spots can be caused by allergic reaction to nail polish, nail hardener, nail glue, or other nail products. Some people are allergic to formaldehyde in nail polish, which can cause white spots, redness, and itching around nails.

Skin conditions like eczema and psoriasis can also affect nails and cause white spots, pitting, and ridges.

**How To Fix**: Stop using the product that caused the reaction. Switch to hypoallergenic, 3-free or 5-free nail polish. Use acetone-free remover. Apply cuticle oil to soothe nails. If you have eczema or psoriasis, follow your doctor's treatment plan.

**Prevention**: Always test new nail products on one nail first. Choose gentle, high-quality products. Use base coat to protect nails from direct contact with polish.

## Cause 5: Excessive Nail Polish Use

Excessive nail polish use, especially without base coat, can cause white spots, yellowing, and dryness. Nail polish and remover dry out nails and can cause surface damage.

If you always wear polish and never give nails a break, your nails become weak and develop white spots and patches.

**How To Fix**: Take a break from polish for 1 to 2 weeks. Always use base coat before color. Use acetone-free remover. Apply cuticle oil daily during breaks. Let nails breathe and rehydrate.

**Prevention**: Give nails 2 to 3 days break every week. Do not leave polish on for more than 7 days. Remove polish gently, never peel it off.

## What Do White Spots Mean?

White spots usually mean your nail was injured a few weeks ago, or your nails need more moisture and nutrients. In most cases, they mean nothing serious.

They are usually harmless and often grow out. But they can sometimes indicate a deficiency or another issue. That is why it is important to look at the pattern.

**One or two small spots on one nail**: Likely injury. Harmless. Will grow out.

**Many spots on many nails**: Could be nutrient deficiency. Improve diet.

White spots that spread, are powdery, and come with thickening: Could be fungal infection. See doctor.

**White spots that cover whole nail**: Could be a health issue. See doctor.

**White spots with pain, swelling, or redness:** Could be infection or injury. See doctor.

## How To Treat And Prevent White Spots

**Keep Your Nails Moisturized**: Apply cuticle oil daily. Moisturized nails are stronger and less likely to get injured. Massage oil into nails and cuticles for 1 minute per hand.

**Eat A Balanced Diet With Zinc And Calcium**: Eat foods rich in zinc, calcium, biotin, iron, and protein. Pumpkin seeds, almonds, milk, yogurt, spinach, eggs, lentils, and chickpeas are excellent for nails. Drink enough water.

**Avoid Harsh Chemicals**: Wear gloves while cleaning. Use acetone-free remover. Choose gentle, high-quality nail products. Harsh chemicals damage nails and cause white spots.

**Use A Gentle Nail Care Routine**: File gently in one direction. Do not cut cuticles. Do not bite or pick nails. Do not peel off gel polish. Be gentle with your nails.

**Give Nails Breaks From Polish**: Take 2 to 3 days break every week. Use base coat always. Remove polish on time.

**Protect Your Nails**: Do not use nails as tools. Wear gloves for heavy work. Be careful not to hit your nails.

## Home Remedies For White Spots

**Coconut Oil Massage**: Massage virgin coconut oil into nails every night. It moisturizes and strengthens nails.

**Lemon And Olive Oil Soak**: Mix 1 tablespoon lemon juice with 2 tablespoons olive oil. Soak nails for 10 minutes, rinse, and moisturize. Do once a week. Lemon brightens nails, olive oil nourishes.

**Milk Soak**: Soak nails in warm milk for 10 minutes. Milk contains calcium and lactic acid which gently nourish nails. Do once a week.

**Vitamin E Oil**: Apply vitamin E oil to nails daily. It repairs damaged nails and promotes healthy growth.

These remedies do not remove white spots instantly, but they help new healthy nail grow and prevent new spots.

## When Should You See A Doctor?

See a doctor if white spots cover the whole nail, appear on all nails suddenly, do not go away after 3 months, spread or get bigger, come with pain, swelling, pus, or bad smell, come with thickening or crumbling of nails, or you have other symptoms like hair fall, fatigue, or pale skin.

Do not try to treat serious nail problems at home. Early treatment can prevent bigger issues.

## Frequently Asked Questions - FAQs

### 1. What causes white spots on nails?

White spots on nails are most commonly caused by minor injury or trauma to the nail matrix, like bumping your nail or aggressive manicuring. Other causes include zinc deficiency, fungal infection, allergic reaction to nail products, and excessive nail polish use. In most cases, they are harmless and grow out on their own.

### 2. Are white spots on nails a sign of calcium deficiency?

No, white spots on nails are rarely caused by calcium deficiency alone. This is a common myth. White spots are more commonly caused by minor injury or zinc deficiency. Calcium is important for nails, but a lack of calcium alone does not usually cause white spots. Eat a balanced diet with zinc, biotin, and calcium for healthy nails.

### 3. How do I get rid of white spots on nails?

You cannot remove white spots instantly because they are inside the nail plate. They will grow out on their own in 2 to 3 months. To help, apply cuticle oil daily, eat zinc and biotin-rich foods, avoid harsh chemicals, be gentle with nails, and take breaks from polish. New healthy nail will grow and replace the spotted nail.

### 4. Can white spots on nails be a sign of fungus?

Yes, white spots can sometimes be a sign of fungal infection, especially if they are powdery, chalky, spreading, and come with thickening or crumbling of nails. Fungal white spots are on the surface and can be scraped off. If you suspect fungus, see a dermatologist for proper treatment. Do not cover fungal nails with polish.

### 5. Should I see a doctor for white spots on nails?

You should see a doctor if white spots cover the whole nail, appear on all nails suddenly, do not go away after 3 months, spread quickly, or come with pain, swelling, or thickening. Most small white spots are harmless and do not need a doctor. But if you are worried, it is always better to get checked.

### 6. How can I prevent white spots on nails?

You can prevent white spots by being gentle with nails, not using nails as tools, wearing gloves for cleaning, eating a balanced diet rich in zinc and biotin, applying cuticle oil daily, using base coat and acetone-free remover, and giving nails regular breaks from polish. Gentle care and good nutrition prevent most white spots.

## Final Thoughts

White spots on nails look scary, but in most cases, they are harmless and often grow out on their own. They are usually a sign of a small injury you forgot about, or a reminder to take better care of your nails and eat a balanced diet.

Do not panic. Do not try to scrub them off aggressively. Just be gentle, keep your nails moisturized, eat zinc and calcium-rich foods, avoid harsh chemicals, and give your nails time to grow healthy again.

If white spots persist, spread, or come with other symptoms, see a dermatologist. Your nails are worth the attention.
