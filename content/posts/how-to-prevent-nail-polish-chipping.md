There's nothing more frustrating than spending an evening on a perfect manicure, only to spot the first chip the very next morning. The good news? Chipping is almost never about the polish itself. It's about **prep, layering and protection**, and all three are easy to fix.

Here are the seven steps nail techs swear by for a chip-free manicure that lasts.

## 1. Start with clean, dry nails

Polish can't grip a surface covered in oil, lotion or old polish residue. Before you start:

- Remove every trace of old polish, even in the corners.
- Wash your hands with soap and dry them completely.
- Swipe each nail with a nail cleanser or rubbing alcohol to remove natural oils.

> Clean nails = better grip for polish. This one step alone can add days to your manicure.

## 2. Never skip the base coat

A quality base coat creates a smooth, slightly sticky surface that polish clings to. It also prevents dark or bright shades from staining your natural nails. Think of it like primer before makeup: invisible, but it makes everything last longer.

## 3. Apply thin layers (not one thick one)

This is the most common mistake. A thick coat looks faster, but it stays soft underneath and peels or dents easily.

1. Apply your first color coat as thin as you can.
2. Wait about two minutes until it's dry to the touch.
3. Add a second thin coat, and a third only if you need more opacity.

Thin layers dry faster, bond better and chip far less.

## 4. Don't forget the nail tips

After each layer, gently swipe the brush horizontally across the free edge (the very tip) of your nail. This is called **capping** or "wrapping the tips," and it seals the edge where most chips begin. It's a tiny step that makes a big difference.

## 5. Seal with a top coat, then reapply it

A good top coat adds shine and a protective shield against scratches, chips and fading. For the longest wear:

- Cap the tips with top coat too.
- Reapply a fresh layer every two to three days to refresh the shine and protection.

## 6. Protect your hands

Hot water, detergent and cleaning products soften polish and break down your top coat.

- Wear gloves when washing dishes or cleaning.
- Avoid harsh chemicals like bleach on bare hands.
- Keep your hands and cuticles moisturized, *after* your polish is fully dry.

## 7. Choose long-lasting formulas

Your products matter too. If you want maximum wear, look for:

| Product | Why it helps |
| --- | --- |
| Long-wear polish | Flexible formulas that resist chips |
| Gel polish | Can last two weeks or more (needs an LED lamp) |
| Quick-dry top coat | Hardens fast, so fewer smudges and dents |

## Extra tips for chip-free nails

- **Don't use your nails as tools.** Opening cans or scratching off labels is the fastest way to chip.
- **Don't bite or pick.** Once a chip starts, picking makes it spread.
- **Keep nails at a healthy length.** Very long nails take more impact.
- **Store polish in a cool, dark place** so it doesn't thicken.
- **Give your nails a break** between manicures to keep them strong.

## The bottom line

A long-lasting manicure comes down to a clean base, thin coats, sealed tips and a little daily protection. Try these steps on your next nail day and you'll notice the difference within a week.
