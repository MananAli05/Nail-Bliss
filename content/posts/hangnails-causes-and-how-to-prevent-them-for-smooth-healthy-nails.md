## Hangnails: Causes And How To Prevent Them For Smooth, Healthy & Beautiful Nails

You are enjoying your beautiful nails, and then suddenly, ouch! A small piece of torn skin around your nail catches on your clothes, your hair, or your sweater. It hurts, it looks messy, and it is so tempting to pull it off.

That painful little piece of skin is called a hangnail. Hangnails are one of the most annoying and painful nail problems, but they are also one of the easiest to prevent and fix if you know what to do.

Many people think hangnails are a part of the nail, but they are not. They are torn skin. Pulling them off can make them worse, cause infection, and damage your skin. The right way is to trim them gently, moisturize, and prevent new ones from forming.

In this complete guide, you will learn what hangnails are, 5 common causes, how to fix them safely at home, how to prevent them, what not to do, and when to see a doctor. By the end, you will know how to keep your nails smooth, healthy, and beautiful.

## What Are Hangnails?

Hangnails are small pieces of torn skin around your nails that can be painful and catch on things.

They usually appear on the sides of the nail or near the cuticle. They are not part of the nail plate. They are part of the skin around the nail, the cuticle or the sidewall, that has dried, cracked, and torn.

Hangnails are small, but they can be very painful. They catch on clothes, hair, towels, and everything you touch. They make your manicure look messy and your hands look unkempt. If you pull them, they can tear deeper, bleed, and get infected.

Hangnails can happen to anyone, but they are more common in people with dry skin, people who bite or pick their nails, people who work with water and chemicals, and in winter when air is dry and cold.

Do not confuse hangnails with ingrown nails. Ingrown nails are when the nail itself grows into the skin, usually on toes. Hangnails are torn skin around fingernails.

## Why Do Hangnails Happen? 5 Common Causes

## Cause 1: Dry Skin - The Number One Cause

Dry skin is the most common cause of hangnails. When the skin around your nails loses moisture, it becomes dry, rough, and brittle. Dry skin cracks easily, and those cracks become hangnails.

Dry skin is caused by cold weather, dry air, hot water, harsh soaps, hand sanitizer, and not using moisturizer.

If your hands feel rough, your cuticles look white and dry, and you get hangnails often, dry skin is the reason.

**How To Fix**: Keep hands moisturized daily. Apply hand cream after every hand wash and cuticle oil 2 to 3 times a day. Use a thick cream with shea butter and glycerin. Drink enough water.

## Cause 2: Frequent Hand Washing

Washing your hands often is good for hygiene, but it is bad for skin around nails if you do not moisturize after. Every time you wash, you strip natural oils from your skin. Without oil, skin becomes dry and cracks.

People who wash hands many times a day, like nurses, teachers, moms, and cooks, get hangnails more often.

**How To Fix**: Use mild, moisturizing soap instead of harsh soap. Use lukewarm water, not hot water. Pat dry gently. Always apply hand cream and cuticle oil immediately after washing. Keep a small hand cream in your bag and at your desk.

## Cause 3: Harsh Chemicals

Harsh chemicals in cleaning products, detergents, bleach, dishwashing soaps, and even nail products can dry out and damage the skin around nails. They strip moisture, irritate skin, and cause cracking and hangnails.

Even acetone nail polish remover is very drying and can cause hangnails.

**How To Fix**: Always wear rubber gloves while cleaning and washing dishes. Use acetone-free remover. Choose gentle, hypoallergenic nail products. Wash hands with mild soap after using chemicals and moisturize immediately.

## Cause 4: Nail Biting Or Picking

Biting or picking your nails and cuticles is a major cause of hangnails. When you bite, you tear the skin around nails unevenly, which creates hangnails. Picking at dry skin also makes it worse.

Once you get one hangnail and pull it, you often create another one next to it. It becomes a vicious cycle.

**How To Fix**: Stop biting and picking. Keep nails short and well-groomed. Keep hands moisturized so there is no dry skin to pick. Keep nails busy with cuticle oil massage instead of picking. If you bite when stressed, use a stress ball or bitter nail polish.

## Cause 5: Cold Weather

Winter is hangnail season. Cold, dry air outside and heated, dry air inside both dry out your skin. Your hands are exposed to cold more than any other part of your body, except your face.

In winter, your cuticles become dry, cracked, and more likely to tear into hangnails. If you go out without gloves, your hands get even drier.

**How To Fix**: Wear warm gloves outside in winter. Use a richer, thicker hand cream in winter. Apply cuticle oil before bed and wear cotton gloves overnight. Use a humidifier at home if air is very dry.

## How To Fix Hangnails Safely At Home - Step By Step

Do NOT pull, tear, or bite off a hangnail. Pulling can tear deeper, cause bleeding, pain, and infection. Here is the safe way to fix it:

### Step 1: Soak Your Hands

Soak your hands in warm water for 5 to 10 minutes. Add a few drops of olive oil or coconut oil to the water. Soaking softens the skin and makes trimming easier and painless. Do not use hot water.

### Step 2: Pat Dry Gently

Pat your hands dry with a soft towel. Do not rub harshly.

### Step 3: Trim Loose Skin Gently

Use clean, sharp nail clippers or cuticle nippers. Do not use your teeth or fingers. Carefully trim only the loose, torn piece of skin. Do not cut healthy skin. Cut close to the base, but do not cut too deep.

If you are not comfortable trimming, you can also just apply cuticle oil and let it heal on its own. Trimming is best if the hangnail is catching on things.

### Step 4: Clean The Area

Wash the area gently with mild soap and water to prevent infection. Pat dry.

### Step 5: Apply Cuticle Oil And Moisturizer

Apply cuticle oil to the area and massage gently. Then apply a thick hand cream. This soothes the skin and helps it heal faster. If the area is red or sore, you can apply a small amount of antibacterial ointment and cover with a bandage for one day.

### Step 6: Do Not Pick Again

Leave it alone. Let it heal. Keep applying oil daily. Do not pick at the area while it heals.

If the hangnail is very painful, red, swollen, or has pus, do not try to fix it at home. See a doctor.

## How To Prevent Hangnails - 5 Proven Ways

### Prevention 1: Moisturize Regularly

This is the most important prevention tip. Moisturized skin does not crack into hangnails.

Apply hand cream after every hand wash. Apply cuticle oil 2 to 3 times a day. Keep one hand cream in your bag, one at your desk, and one beside your bed.

Use a cream with shea butter, glycerin, vitamin E, and ceramides. For very dry skin, use a thick ointment or balm at night.

Make moisturizing a daily habit, like brushing teeth. Do not wait until your hands are already dry and cracked.

### Prevention 2: Use Cuticle Oil Daily

Cuticle oil is your best friend for preventing hangnails. It keeps the skin around nails soft, flexible, and healthy.

Use jojoba oil, coconut oil, almond oil, or vitamin E oil. Apply one drop per nail and massage for 1 minute per hand. Massage improves blood circulation and keeps cuticles healthy.

Apply cuticle oil in the morning, after hand washing, and before bed. Nighttime is best because oil has 6 to 8 hours to work while you sleep.

If you do not have cuticle oil, use pure coconut oil or olive oil. They work just as well.

### Prevention 3: Wear Gloves For Household Work

Wear rubber gloves while washing dishes, cleaning, gardening, or doing any work with water and chemicals. Gloves protect your hands from drying and damage.

Wear warm gloves in cold weather. Cold air dries out skin quickly.

Keep a pair of rubber gloves in your kitchen and bathroom so you never forget. Prevention is much easier than treatment.

### Prevention 4: Trim Loose Skin Gently

Do not pull loose skin. If you see a small piece of dry skin starting to lift, trim it gently with clean clippers before it becomes a painful hangnail.

Keep your nails well-groomed. File gently, keep nails clean, and push back cuticles gently after soaking. Do not cut cuticles, as cutting can cause hangnails and infection.

Grooming once a week prevents most hangnails.

### Prevention 5: Eat A Balanced Diet

Healthy skin starts from inside. Eat foods rich in vitamin E, vitamin C, biotin, zinc, and healthy fats. These nutrients keep your skin soft, flexible, and healthy.

Eat almonds, walnuts, avocados, salmon, eggs, spinach, citrus fruits, and sweet potatoes. Drink 7 to 8 glasses of water daily. Hydrated skin is less likely to crack.

Avoid excessive sugar and processed foods, as they can cause inflammation and dry skin.

Extra Tips To Keep Nails Smooth, Healthy And Beautiful

Avoid biting your nails. Biting tears skin and creates hangnails. Keep nails short if you bite when stressed.

Keep nails well-groomed. File gently in one direction, clean under nails, and moisturize daily. Well-groomed nails look beautiful and stay healthy.

Use a gentle hand cream. Choose a fragrance-free, moisturizing cream if you have sensitive skin. Avoid harsh, alcohol-based lotions.

Do not cut cuticles. Cuticles protect nails from infection. Cutting them causes hangnails and infection. Push them back gently instead.

Do not peel off polish. Peeling removes layers of nail and tears skin around nails. Use acetone-free remover.

Give nails breaks. Take 2 to 3 days break from polish every week. Let nails breathe and rehydrate.

## Common Mistakes To Avoid With Hangnails

**Mistake 1:** Pulling or tearing hangnails. This makes them worse and causes infection.

**Mistake 2:** Biting hangnails. This tears skin unevenly and introduces bacteria.

**Mistake 3:** Cutting too deep. Cutting healthy skin causes pain and infection.

**Mistake 4:** Skipping moisturizer. Dry skin always leads to hangnails.

**Mistake 5:** Not wearing gloves. Chemicals and water damage skin.

**Mistake 6:** Ignoring infection signs. Redness, swelling, pus, and severe pain need medical attention.

Avoid these mistakes and you will get fewer hangnails.

## When Should You See A Doctor?

Most hangnails can be treated at home. But see a doctor if:

The area around the hangnail is very red, swollen, warm, and painful. You see pus or yellow discharge. You have a fever. The hangnail keeps coming back in the same spot. The skin around nails is very thick, cracked, or bleeding. You have diabetes and get frequent hangnails or infections.

People with diabetes need to be extra careful with hangnails because infections can become serious quickly.

Do not ignore signs of infection. Early treatment prevents bigger problems.

## Frequently Asked Questions - FAQs

### 1. What causes hangnails?

Hangnails are caused by dry skin, frequent hand washing, harsh chemicals, nail biting or picking, and cold weather. When the skin around your nails becomes dry and brittle, it cracks and tears into hangnails. Lack of moisture is the main cause. Keeping hands moisturized prevents most hangnails.

### 2. Should I pull off a hangnail?

No, you should never pull off a hangnail. Pulling can tear deeper, cause bleeding, pain, and infection. Instead, soak your hands in warm water for 5 minutes, then trim the loose skin gently with clean nail clippers. Apply cuticle oil and hand cream after trimming to help it heal.

### 3. How do I get rid of hangnails fast?

To get rid of hangnails fast, soak hands in warm water with a few drops of oil for 5 to 10 minutes, trim the loose skin gently with clean clippers, clean the area with mild soap, and apply cuticle oil and hand cream. Keep the area moisturized and do not pick at it. It will heal in 1 to 2 days.

### 4. How can I prevent hangnails?

You can prevent hangnails by moisturizing hands daily, using cuticle oil 2 to 3 times a day, wearing gloves while cleaning and in cold weather, eating a balanced diet, not biting or picking nails, and trimming loose skin gently before it tears. Consistent moisturizing is the best prevention.

### 5. Are hangnails a sign of vitamin deficiency?

Hangnails are usually caused by dryness, not vitamin deficiency. But lack of vitamin E, vitamin C, biotin, and zinc can make skin drier and more likely to crack. Eating a balanced diet with nuts, avocados, citrus fruits, and eggs helps keep skin healthy and prevents hangnails.

### 6. When should I see a doctor for a hangnail?

You should see a doctor if the area is very red, swollen, warm, painful, has pus, or you have a fever. Also see a doctor if hangnails keep coming back in the same spot or do not heal in 3 days. People with diabetes should see a doctor sooner for any nail infection.
